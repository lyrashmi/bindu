Tripura is both embodying all the triadic forms, as well as transcending the triadic forms. Tripura is simultaneously expressing the cohabitiveness of the divine in these expressed forms and the transcendence of the divine of all these expressed manifolds. The upanishads talk about the transcendence of this triadic structure primarily from within the context of transcending our waking (jagrad), dreaming (svopna) and deep sleep (shushupti) states. The fourth being turiya. 

Saundarya Lahari (by Shankaraacharya), verse 41 is where Shankaracharya lays the foundation for the sixfold meditation of the Shambhava wisdoms, all coming from the Kubjika pantheon - and concludes the samaya practice in the muladhara chakra with the invocation of Navatma bhairava in the form of samaya. https://shlokam.org/texts/saundarya-41/
This is what creates the sixfold structure of the six Shambhava wisdoms taught within the pantheon of Kubjika that elevates the original triadic structure going into the intersecting triangles. The same Pramātṛ (subject, or person to which something is revealed), prameya (object of comprehension), Pramāṇa (means of cognition) reflected two-fold in the illumination aspect (prakāśa) and reflection aspect (vimarśa) becomes a hexagon via the intersection of two triangles. And this hexagonal structure is what forms the basis for the samaya reflection. Within this integration of samaya that has navatmabhairava and bhairavi. This is where we find samarasya (rasa=the juice/what flows, also aesthetic experience, rasa is severing//sama=mingling/mixed equally) representing ultimate balance, equilibrium of opposites. Sometimes visualised erotically, most times just sitting on the lap of bhairava. The world is rapture right now, in front of our eyes right now is the very expression of navatmabhairava and navetmaveshwari in the absolute interpenetration, which self-affirms this consciousness. This is what constitutes the absolute singularity. An active advaita, an active non-dualism, an active singularity. 

Aham: A (the absolute) referring to Shiva, Ha (the rays of the absolute) referring to Shakti and Ma (anu) referring to my individuated state of being. Tripura is this triad. This Tripura sadhana becomes a means/ a medium for reaffirming the teachings that you can find in the upanishads.

Tripura is prior to the phonetic manifestation. She is also the union of [[Prakāśa (प्रकाश)]] / [[Vimarśa (विमर्श)]] and is therefore called Kāma-kala as well. She is an integration of the energies of the triadic structure. Tripura is the consciousness-basis of iccha, jnana, kriya. 

Tripura circumscribing while transcending both atmatattva, [[Vidyā (विद्या)|vidya]]tattva and [[Śiva (शिव)|shiva]]tattva. She is therefore beyond the abode (dhāma), in dhāma we go to kāmarūpa (form of desire, shakta [[Pīṭha (पीठ)]] in Assam) [[Rāga (राग)]] , pūrnagiri (full mountain, shakta pīṭha in Uttarakhand), jālandhara (net holder/ one who spreads net, shakta pīṭha in Punjab), and then dhyāna (the fourth pitha) transcending - but then also residing in all of these three pithas.

Viyat cakra, dahara akasha: The Sri Cakra is visualised inside the thousand petalled lotus above the forehead. And it is visualised as the self-expressing empty space of consciousness, as the manifestation of Sri Yantra, which you can trace in the Heart, or yet again up in the same Viyat centre, within the core of the thousand petalled lotus.

She exists as Ṣoḍaśī in the form of the mahashodashi mantra, in which we also find the smaller baalaa mantra, amongst others. She also exists as Rakta Kali (red Kali), amongst others.
