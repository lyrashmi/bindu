Puryaṣṭaka literally means “eight-fold city” and it refers to the [[Sūkṣma-Sthūla (सूक्ष्म-स्थूल)|sukṣma]]-śarira, or the subtle body ([[Sūkṣma-Sthūla (सूक्ष्म-स्थूल)|sthūla]]-śarira being the gross body) which the individual soul (anu, atman, paśu) inhabits in the physical body and is its vehicle when it transmigrates. In Aham, the M that represents the individuated state of being is the puryaṣṭaka (the city if eight), according to Acharyaji this is also the perfect fusion of shiva-shakti/ prakasha-vimarsha (where the navarasas occur in the resulting nectar). There are various views concerning its nature. According to the most common one, it consists of the five sensations of form, sound, smell, taste, and touch and the internal mental organ consisting of the mind ([[Manas (मनस्)]]), intellect (_buddhi_) and ego (_ahaṃkāra_). However here is an edited version which is mainly based on Śankaras advaita doctrine:
1. The five tanmātras - subtle elements
2. The fives thulabhutas (pañca pahābhūtas) - gross elements.
3. The five jñānendriyas - organs of sense.
4. The five karmendriyas - organs of action.
5. The antahkaraṇa - or the inner organ comprising [[Manas (मनस्)]] (mind), buddhi (intellect) and ahaṃkāra (ego-sense).
6. The trinity, such as the guṇas - sattva, rajas and tamas.
7. The pradhāna (pra=before, dhāna=substrate/essence; the primordial matter, or root cause of the universe) or prakriti.
8. The five [[Kañcuka (कञ्चुक)|kañcukas]].
According to Abhinavagupta the eight elements of the subtle body are the fivefold vital breath, the group of cognitive sense faculties, the group of active sense faculties and the intellect.

Technically there are three bodies: Sthūla (gross), Sūkṣma (subtle) and [[Kāraṇaśarīra (कारणशरीर)|Kāraṇa]] (causal).
