 lit. ‘proceeding, flowing in steps’. Denotes the sequence of the phases of manifestation. The term is commonly used in this sense in the [[Tantra (तन्त्र)|tantras]] of the Kālīkrama. The Krama system deals with a synthesis of the Śaiva philosophy, the Śākta esotericism and tantric concepts of life. From it we get the [[Mahāvidyā (महाविद्या)]] as well as Caṇḍika worship (see: [[Ugracaṇḍā (उग्रचण्डा)]]). In some cases we can think of the word just referring to a specific sequence of initiations (see: [[Dīkṣā (दीक्षा)]]). This system teaches that step-by-step succession makes your realisation firm (as opposed to [[Śaktipāta (शक्तिपात)]]). In its sense of divine manifestation, the Kālīkrama’s cosmic process (in its five-fold variant, [[Pañcavāha (पञ्चवाह)]]), which the sequence of actions within a ritual (see: [[Pūjā (पूजा)]]) mirrors, looks as follows:

1. Sṛṣṭikrama (सृष्टिक्रम) - “Krama of emanation” [[Vāgbhāva (वाग्भाव)]]
2. Sthitikrama (स्थितिक्रम) - “Krama of persistence” [[Samarasya (समरस्य)]]
3. Saṃhārakrama (संहारक्रम) - “Krama of absorbtion”
4. Anākhyakrama (अनाख्यक्रम) - “The inexplicable krama”, which includes all three previous ones in oneness. Is identified as [[Cāmuṇḍā (चामुण्डा)]], [[Kālasaṃkarṣiṇī (कालसंकर्षिणी)]], as well as nigraha.
5. Illumination (_bhāsākrama_) in which the deity and its essential, pure conscious nature manifest.

[[Ugracaṇḍā (उग्रचण्डा)|Chandika]] is identified as both Anākhya and [[Śiva (शिव)|Bhāsā]], whereas [[Cāmuṇḍā (चामुण्डा)]], being her śakti (power) is only Anākhya.

See also: [[Mahārtha (महार्थ)]]

Prameya (the object), pramana (the means of knowledge) and pramata (the subject) are each subject to 4 stages of successive (krama) emanation (with bhāsa being the 13th, the one where there is no temporal succession: The shrishtikrama (krama of outpouring/ emanation [[Vāgbhāva (वाग्भाव)|vāgbhava]]), the sthithikrama (the krama of persistence, also called the ‘heart of the Yogini’ in which we find samarasya, or the preceding and succeeding combined), the shrishtikrama (krama of dissolution) and anākhya, or inexplicable/ unnamable krama where all three are present in oneness. These 4 kramas applied to prameya, pramana and pramata respectively are what create the 12 Kalis surrounding Kalasamkarshini. (The Kramasadbhāva extends these 12 to 17 (the 17th again being the unity of all) and the rest of the 16 each having 4 [[Yoginī (योगिनी)]], making a total of 64).

Another way to understand the ritualised mirroring of the kramas is through the concepts of ṛta and satya (the natural order of truth and absolute truth), where ṛta is the ritual order, manifested order in nature and society as well as the dynamic (pulsating) order of the absolute (see: [[Spanda (स्पन्द)]]) while satya is the changeless absolute (kūṭastha).

Her emptiness is an expression of the world, her perfection/completion is the dissolution of the world and experiencing in the form of singularity. She is constantly playing this two-fold nature of self-expression and unfolding (vistara) and infolding (pravritti) of externality. This is the recognition of shakti, in the form of kali. After dissolution we call it anākhya, the inexpressible state that is nameless and then bhasa, the luminosity where teemingly some externalities start appearing to restart the wheel. 
There is this 5-fold structure, associated with Shakti (as opposed to the 5-fold acts of [[Śiva (शिव)]]) known as Abhāsana (manifestation or illumination), Rakti (attachment, or engagement within the externalised forms), Vimarśana (reflection, or reflexively cognising those manifest forms that you are engaged with), Bijavasthapana (and then preserving some seeds in the form of the habits, or memory), Vilapana (and then dissolving all other forms into pure consciousness). Every day and every instance consciousness is constantly functioning in this 5-fold structure. (Useful to note here as well is the advaita doctrine of drishti-shrishti; appearance-creation, which says that seeing and creation are one and the same).

Within the 5-fold structure of Kali ([[Pañcavāha (पञ्चवाह)]]), we do not just go and transcend this triadic structure. We start thinking about this same reality in a new way (in the form of pure consciousness), it's blissful modes (Anākhyakrama and Bhāsākrama) and volition (iccha, Sthitikrama [persistence]), cognition (jnana, Sṛṣṭikrama [emanation]), action (kriya, Saṃhārakrama [absorbtion]). A completely new set of reflection. Within it we get the creative pulsation ([[Spanda (स्पन्द)]]) as the pulsation of the absolute shiva, which is not a directional flowing, but rather a recognition of its simultaneous presence and transcendence. Within this simultaneous presence and transcendence shakti allows itself to recognise into the manifold. Vedanta teaches us that we should transcend the triadic structure. This 5-fold structure however teaches us that we should come back and reaffirm our own presence in the 4-fold structure (the fifth being luminosity; bhasa).
