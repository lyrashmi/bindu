Vowel.

The 16 vowels are the [[Bhairava (भैरव)|Bhairavas]] (starting with Amrita, or A), culminating in visarga (:), or absolute potential - the consonants are the rudras (starting with Jayarudra or Ka, with the last phoneme being Ksha).

Mātrikā order begins with A and ends with Ksha (the fifty bhairavas correspond to the 50 phonemes in the Mātrikā order). Mālinī order begins with the phoneme Na, and ends with Pha.
##### **The 16 vowels** 
*(the amrita, lit. “without (a) death (mrita)”, “undying”)*
**A** (अ) is Amritabhairava (at the center of the 16 powers in the wheel of Aghora), the first throb of being established in the state of transcending the mind (unmanā). *Remember, the wheel of prameya (the object) has 16 spokes. These are the same 16 powers of the moon that culminate in visarga (the vowels).*

**Ā** (आ) is Amritapūrna who is identical to [nija-]ānanda (the surge of bliss or the expression of fullness, i.e. pūrna). Recognised as the constructed state of the absolute (anuttara). 

**I** (इ) is Amritābha who is suffused with the power of volition, who is skilled in creating an endless number of worlds. Nirānanda.

**Ī** (ई) is Amritadrava who is identical to the principle of Īśvara. Who pacifies all forms of heat (or all suffering), who is the counter-image of the absolute (A) and who is resting on the state of the blossoming of boundless volition, and who wears the moon or the rays of nectar as his crest jewel.

**U** (उ) is Amritaugha who is the expressive mode of Īśvara (Ī), who is replete with volition or the expressed state of fullness due to the recognition of the absolute (A).

**Ū** (ऊ) is Amritormi who is the ocean of consciousness who is splashing with the tides of expression (or the state of bliss in the form of mahānanda), where the entirety of the tides (or the phoneme Ū that manifests by being the phoneme U combined) that is expressing (or manifesting the phoneme U) the principle of Īśvara, an embodiment  of volition (or composed of the phoneme I), which in turn is the mass of bliss (or the expression of the phoneme Ā) corresponding to (the absolute) "I-am" (or pūrnāhantā).

**Ri** (ऋ) is Amritasyandana - the great ocean of Bhairava consciousness (or the consciousness of the totality as I-am) that is the essence of the flow of nectar. He is the Lord who is replete with the rasa of consciousness (representing the first of the nectar-representing phonemes of Ri), (or replete with Cidānanda, the sixth state of bliss) caused by the perturbed tides (or the expression of the phoneme Ū) that are propelled by the expansion of the self (represented by A).

**Rii** (ॠ) is Amritāngada who bestows the nectar body in absolute fullness (representing jagadānanda, or the blossoming, or the bliss of totality) that comes to repose after having reached the seventh state of the nectar (Ri), which in the initial state was the very absolute state (A).

**Lri** (ऌ) is Amritavapus who adorns the third part of the four aspects of nectar (or the third among the four Amrita vowels) who manifests manifold successions, even where there is no succession.

**Lrii** (ॡ) is Amritodgāra, who brings awakening to all entities that are emaciated by the poison of defects (of the loss of self-identity as Śiva), being determined by the fourth aspect of the nectar (or the tenth phoneme).

**E** (ए) is Amritāsya, who has assumed the form of the means of cognition (or who has become the pramāna) of the entirety of the world, who is of the character of bliss that is replete with the triadic potencies (expressed by the triadic deities Parā, Parāparā, and Aparā), (or who represents the triangular structure) that initiates with the absolute (or the phoneme A) when it is merged with volition, or the phoneme I.

**Ai** (ऐ) is Amritatanu, the remover (of all suffering), who is the absolute cause for the flow, the awakening that "I am not determined within the epidermis" (atanubodha), the very "I-am" that is expressed in every single form (or that encompasses everything) composed with the fusion of the triadic potencies (or the expressed form of the phoneme E represented by Ai), by means of the aspects whose essential nature is oneness (Ai).

**O** (ओ) is Amritasecana, who is saturated with nectar, who himself emerges in the state of the absolute (or the initial phoneme of A), who replenishes everything (by means of the vowel O) with his own rasa and transforms all into Śiva nature.

**Au** (औ) is Amritamūrt, the embodiment of ambrosia, who is the essential nature of the saturated form of the triadic potencies (expressed in the phoneme of O), who carries a trident that destroys threefold snares (of rāga, dvesha and moha), and who is the healing medicine (or the phoneme Au) for mental and physical suffering.

**Am** (अं) is Amritrśa, who brings the entirety of the entities to be cognised to absolute fullness within its own state of the absolute within a singular drop (signified by the vowel Am and marked by a single drop .), having transformed them into the fluid of nectar.

Ah (अः) is Amritadhara (represented by the last vowel ":"), who emits the world in the initial stage internally and externally, (as expressed by the two drops) that flow from its initial form of the absolute (represented by the phoneme A) in the sequence of the expression of bliss, marked by the phoneme Aa.
