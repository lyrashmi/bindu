lit. sitting near (having close proximity to the divine). Worship. (e.g. yantra upasana).

See also: [[Bhāvanā (भावना)]], [[Pūjā (पूजा)]]

* Nirguna Upasana (निर्गुण उपासना) refers to the worship or contemplation of the Divine in its formless, attributeless essence, beyond all qualities ([[Guṇa (गुण)]]). 
* Saguna upasana is with form, or qualities ([[Guṇa (गुण)]]). 

The very first beings, from an evolutionary perspective, the very first sense of perceiving the world, the very first channels through which they experienced the manifested world, was touch. And hence, touching is an essential part of ritual worship. You touch the mother's feet, you touch the deity, because it is an embodiment of the divine. This is comparable with the successive manifestation via the chakras. Anahata is the first sense perception of touch, which contains also all potentialities within the heart.
Even within a cellular perspective the first sensation we have is a tactile sensation.

The rishis merged sight and hearing, which is what mantra is. Tantra is merging all sensory faculties to experience a blend of singular consciousness, which is what the worship of Kali is.
The most real experience, the most powerful experience, is not the experience of depriving the sensory faculties or the sensory conduits to reach this transcendent absolute, but rather to utilise all of the sensory faculties in the act of the divine encounter. This is what happens during puja, all sensory conduits are active at the same time.
By depriving yourself of the food that you cooked for so long and offering it to the divine, you are testing yourself, and if you are then able to activate this totality in the context of puja, you will encounter the divine as Vishwaroopa, as the universal form  through which the divine unfolds. But also at the same time you will have the experience of the absolute transcendent. Because the one doesn’t negate this other.

The object is always defined by the subject. The subject always has the power over the object, which means that as long as God is an object within the mind of the subject, the subject hasn't gone deep enough, because at that point God is inferior to the subject. So the subject needs to surrender to the totality.
So the progression is going from the object-subject experience to the blending of the subject-object experience, i.e. Shiva and me blending, then further on to a singular experience.
And at that point, you are gone, you don’t exist. Your entire existence, your memories, your corporeal reality, your ego, your social identity, your thought processes, none of that means anything. You **are** the divine.
But with this, we don't mean rejection. This problem about not rejecting the world... The manifested world resolves itself. Because this ego, these things that make you you, are the embodiment of emptiness. They are Kali. They are Bhadrakali. So, in that sense, we have the negation, without actively rejecting (nor accepting). i.e. It is not a negation of the body, it is about focussed on something higher to encourage the revelation of something which lies beneath the body.
