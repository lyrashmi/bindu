Just cause you eat sattvika/good food doesn’t mean it cultivates virtue for you, it is simply what you should be doing. There are 5 karmas (actions), which are considered **nītya** (lit. ‘everlasting, eternal’, in moral conduct: those ethics that are consistent and universal // in rituals: daily obligatory duties or practices that sustain spiritual discipline), also called Pancha Mahayajnas (five great sacrifices):
1. Brahma Yajna (worship of/ sacrifice to knowledge): Dedicated to Brahman, reflection on the mantras and your own universal being
2. Deva Yajna (worship of/ sacrifice to the deities): Fire offerings, libations (pouring of liquid offering), mantra japa
3. Pitri Yajna (worship of/ sacrifice to ancestors): Any offering of gratitude or oblations (tarpana) to honouring your ancestors
4. Manushya Yajna (sacrifice you make for the sake of humanity): Separate something from your daily consumption for others
5. Bhuta Yajna (service to beings/ animals): Separate something from your daily intake for the animals
These five are **mandatory**! There is no choice, if you do not do it there is vice, if you do not do it you are not cultivating special virtues in form of sattva.

Then there are **naimittika**, the occasional or conditional duties, which are performed on special occasions. These include things like death rites (shraddha), yearly rituals, marriage rituals etc.
