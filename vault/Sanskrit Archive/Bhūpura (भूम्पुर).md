The term bhūpura refers to the outermost boundary or enclosure of a yantra or mandala, typically represented as a square with four gates, one on each side. This square symbolizes the material, manifest world and serves as the starting point for spiritual ascent through the layers of the mandala toward the central [[Bindu (बिन्दु)]] (the point of unity or ultimate reality). The gates often signify openings through which the seeker enters the sacred domain, transitioning from the mundane to the divine.

Bhūpura in Tantra

1. Cosmic Boundary: It marks the boundary between the external, worldly realm (samsara) and the internal, sacred space of the mandala, encapsulating both duality and multiplicity within a structured framework.

2. Progression of Sadhana: The bhūpura is where the spiritual journey begins. The aspirant moves inward, passing through successive layers (symbolic of the tattvas or elements of reality) toward higher states of consciousness.

3. Protection and Limitation: It serves both as a protective enclosure for the sacred space and as a symbolic representation of limitation—the finite nature of the world, which must be transcended
