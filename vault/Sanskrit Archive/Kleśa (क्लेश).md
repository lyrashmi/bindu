kleśāḥ (क्लेशाः, plural) refer to the mental and emotional afflictions or obstacles that cause suffering and prevent spiritual progress.
In the Yoga Sutras of Patanjali, the kleshas are described as the root causes of human suffering. They cloud the mind and keep individuals bound to the cycle of birth and rebirth (samsara). Patanjali identifies five primary kleshas: 
1. Avidya (Ignorance)
2. Asmita (Egoism)
3. Raga (Attachment)
4. Dvesha (Aversion)
5. Abhinivesha (Fear of Death)

What we do in our life's and in what way we suffer is determined by the kleshas. Kleshas constitute [[Saṃskāra (संस्कार)|samskaras]], samskaras create new karmas, new karmas create new samskaras. We are in this vicious cycle, a never ending circle of reconstitution of karmas and their consumption, and then new karmas. As long as we allow our sensory faculties [[Indra (इन्द्र)]] to roam and be engaged, we are superimposing ourselves and allowing that self-imposed self in the intellect to flow through the sensory faculties and enjoy the objects, we are cultivating new and new karmas. And that cultivation of new and new karmas will need to have a new consumption that will require a new body.
