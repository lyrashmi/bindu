According to Abhinavagupta both duality (dvaita) and non-duality (advaita) are part of an all encompassing singular mega category called absolute non-duality (paramadvaita). It argues for absolute inclusiveness; we don't have a pot and a non-pot (neither the pot is not a non-pot, nor the non-pot isn't not a pot), but both are singular manifestations in a plural form of a singular principle, i.e. non-dual consciousness - in Sarvamnaya this category would be defined by Mahartha. In contemporary terminology this form of non-dualism could be called pluralistic non-dualism, or subjective pluralism. Does this mean we are left with inconclusive evidence of non-dual reality; i.e. subject-consciousness and object-consciousness duality? Abhinavagupta would respond to that: The process of manifesting (abhaasana) both to subject-consciousness and object-consciousness is a singular process and hence is non-dual.
