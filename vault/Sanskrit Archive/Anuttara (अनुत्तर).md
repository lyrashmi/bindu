Meaning of Anuttara:
According to Abhinava it is that which has nothing (an) higher (uttara) [beyond it], and of which there is nothing higher.
A (the power which rests on the supreme light of consciousness beyond māyā, as such being the beginning and end of the cycle of the supreme awareness of aham), nut (inclination to creative action), tara (dominance over all things). Thus Anuttara is that in which power (A), inclined to action, dominates over all things. Nut, specifically is: to propel in the sense of action characterised by spatial succession. Anuttara is unbounded, unmeasurable space and so is unbounded space and so is devoid of any such action. The only activity of the Anuttara which is the light of consciousness is its reflective awareness. All objectivity is one with Anuttara. It is the ground of the power of consciousness (citshakti), which wields the trident of the three energies of will, knowledge and action. Thus, from anuttara proceeds the great emanation (mahāshrishti) which encompasses all the cycles of creation and destruction. One with Anuttara (A), the universe (Ha) is the dynamism of the self-perpetuating ‘I’ (Aham) consciousness that flows out from it into individual consciousness (M), and from there it returns back to be reabsorbed into it (A).
