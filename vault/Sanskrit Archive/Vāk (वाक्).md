The levels of speech:
• Vaikhari Vāk – Most external speech (action)
• Madhyamā Vāk – Intermediate level of speech (not articulated out, but you can hear it)
• Pashyantī Vāk – That you can only see intuitively (words are there, but they are still separated from their reference)
• Parā Vāk – Not separated from its reference (it is one and the same)

Vāk is the zeal of [[Spanda (स्पन्द)]] that expresses in the form of speech.

It is not conceivable to think of subject or object without this foundational Parā, which is both pure consciousness and the speech as it is in her most pristine form. Then when she manifests outside - the zeal (sistriksha) to express and become manifold unfolds in two structures: In one there is an externalisation, but in that externalisation there is not given a sense of the out, it is still one within the self. It is the self-saying speech called Pashyanti. And when that same speech can be objectified and the thoughts can be construed, when you as the subject manage to differentiate yourself from your thoughts, then those thoughts are in the Madhyama level. These both are in this intermediate ground, and these both are governed by the state that Abhinavagupta identifies as Parāparā. This very pure consciousness now fully objectified is in the expressed Vaikhari level of speech. This is where the object is presented to consciousness as an object - as inanimate, as inertia, as the outside. And that outside referred to in words in form of language, which is Vaikhari, we call Aparā. Within this also manifests Pramatri (the subject) - Parā; Pramana (the means of cognition) - Parāparā; Prameya (the external objects) - Aparā.

When you invision the rising, enduring and collapsing and the fourth stage in the beginning of the new stage in these 3 steps of subjective consciousness, in the rise of cognitive faculties and cognitive function and the rise of the object, and an instance where there is neither the rise of the object, nor the rise of the cognitive function, then those are the 12 Kalīs. These 12 Kalīs Abhinavagupta says are worshipped in the Anākhya cakra. Parāparā has everything within subjectivity and objectivity, making her the highest. Her mantra is the longest and in her the eight [[Mātṛkā (मातृका)|matrikas]] are integrated.

In Vaikhari speech is manifest in the form of the exalted word. In parapara (madhyama) speech is manifest in the form of memory. In pashyanti it illumines only the artha (the external entity), as a very subtle level; it is sisthriksha (the desire to create something, to see something); in this she is the signifier and the signified, the language and what is being described by language. And only the brahman remains in the absolute (para) level of speech. So how can you describe the goddess, who herself is the speech? She is sarvabhuta (she who manifests in the form of everything).

Para (the supreme, unmanifest stage of sound), Pashyanti (originating in the heart as “yam”), Madhyama (the intermediate mental stage of sound), Vaikhari (the gross, audible stage of articulated speech). At the Pashyanti stage, sound is not yet expressed verbally but exists as unexpressed potential in the heart (hence Mahalakshmi is connected to iccha, the pure divine will to express and unfold into the manifold).

All expressions are expression by the means of speech. There is a primacy of words, of speech, of phonemes. What we discover within this primacy is pure consciousness expressing itself in the form of speech. When you really want to say something, but you don't have the words (you feel this pure will expressing) that is Parā expressing herself. (as one of the four stages of sound). It manifests in the triadic structure as pashyanti, in which your speech is divided equally within the externalised and internalised.

Mantric birth is the real birth, as we're being infused with pure speech.
