lit. time

The very constitution of samaya (sam=together, aya=to go, coming// coming together in unity), comes from the pantheon of Kubjika. (Samaya is also the vow between guru and practitioner). We are using samaya also as a word for time (in Hindi). In Sanskrit the root "Aya" also means to know/to realise, so samaya is not only that which moves perfectly, it is time that has the absolute movement. Therefore samaya is the absolute flow. But in our Sarvamnaya context we are using samaya as the absolute realisation, not absolute time (or the internalisation of externalised rituals). Going more technically, in our particular case we call a deity herself in the form of Bhairava as samaya, and Tripura Sundari as samayā and the recognition of their union/singularity as a conduit of samayachara or samayāchara (just like the senses are conduits, or channels through which the experience of external manifestation flows), and that is where the meditation begins.
