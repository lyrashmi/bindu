Sound (arisen from [[Dhvani (ध्वनि)]]). It is said to be of five kinds: subtle (sūkṣma), very subtle (susūkṣma), manifest (vyakta), unmanifest (avyakta), and artificial (kṛtrima). Sitting at the lower half-portion of the self, it brings about emanation.

**Anāhata Nāda (अनाहत नाद)** - lit. ‘Unstruck sound’. The pure energy of transcendental
consciousness in its unaltered form. The canvas of “white noise” from which all other sounds emanate. Typically associated with the seed syllable ([[Bīja (बीज)]]) Oṃ.

**Āhata Nāda (आहत नाद)** - lit. ‘Beaten sound, struck sound’, like a drum (see: [[Spanda (स्पन्द)]]). Associated with Devī and her powers (Śakti) and the seed syllable (bīja) Hrīṃ.  

Though the difference between ahata and anahata nada are in degree, not category.

Going further one can divide it like this:
**Ahata** (struck)
**Anahatahata** 
**Anahata** (unstruck)
**Anahatahata utīrna** (the ultimate shabda; the shunya, the soundless sound, echoless echo) transcendent to this whole structure. Beyond the heart-centre.

**Haṃsa (हंस)** - The form of unstruck sound. That supreme seed-syllable (bīja) is located in the heart. Without it there is no perception of ultimate reality and one knows nothing.

[[Bindu (बिन्दु)]] has Gauri [[Śakti (शक्ति)]], nada has Jheshta emanating, vāma from [[Bīja (बीज)]]. These three aspects are visualised as emanating from the triadic bindu nada bija. These three give rise to rudra, rama and vishnu. So this is the very triadic structure of the rishis brahma vishnu rudra. 
Iccha jnana kriya, depicting agni, surya and new moon. There arises the rava from the nada, and that is what is called the shambhda brahman.
