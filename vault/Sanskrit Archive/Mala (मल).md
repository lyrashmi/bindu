**Malas** are specific to Shaivism and non-dual tantric philosophies. 
Literally “Impurities” or veils that obscure the true nature of the self (atman) or ultimate reality (Shiva).

The three primary malas:
1. Anava Mala: The impurity of limitation or finitude, leading to feelings of separateness and inadequacy. Stems from anu (individual atomic in size). Creating attachment and passion [[Rāga (राग)]]
2. Mayiya Mala: The impurity of illusion, stemming from the association with maya (to measure out). Creating aversion ([[Dveṣa (द्वेष)]])
3. Karma Mala: The impurity of action, which binds the soul to the fruits of actions and creates karmic cycles. Creating [[Moha (मोह)]] 

Vimala = without mala. Pure consciousness is without mala and is therefore the perfect surace for reflection (see: [[Abhāsa-Pratibimba (अभास-प्रतिबिम्ब)]]).

The three (or seven) [[Paśa (पश)]] (being in the cocoon) give us mala. (E.g. they create the conditions for the malas to arise). The mala is a colour that taints us, we experience the world with a sense of the mala (like sunglasses). Everyone experiences themselves as limited (anu), then we impose our limitations about ourselves onto others and the world and become fragmented pieces. If the entirety of Shiva and me were to be considered a single mirror, now we are these little fragmented pieces. Everything is reflecting something, but nothing is giving the total picture. A little bit of happiness here, a little bit of joy there, a little bit of bitterness here - because these tiny fragmented pieces of glass can never reflect the totality. And then we assume we know the world best based on these fragmented pieces of perception; and therefore we can never **really** be happy. 

The cocktail/mixture of all conditionings (malas) is what’s called [[Vaicitrya (वैचित्र्य)]].
