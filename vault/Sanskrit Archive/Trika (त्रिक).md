- lit. ‘Threefold’. Another word for Kashmir Shaivism, a tantric tradition whose main founder is Abhinavagupta, and is less focused on ritual and more on householder sādhana. The Tri (three) refers to the system of three goddesses. In Sarvāmnāya (all transmissions), Parā is the goddess revealed through Śiva’s eastward face (Tatpuruṣa). 

1. Parā (परा) - The highest energy (Aghora Śakti). Non-dual (ultimate reality)
2. Aparā (अपर) - The lowest energy (Ghoratari Śakti). Dual (matter)
3. Parāparā (परापर) - The energy combined (Ghorā Śakti). Dual & Non-dual (the self) 

[[Paramadvaita (परमद्वैत)]]

The support is the fire (agni tattva), the cup is the sun (surya tattva), and the pouring of the libation is the moon (soma tattva).

In the "eastern" or "ancient" transmission of Täntrik Saivism (purvamnaya), there are three central Goddess known as the devitraya or the Trika (the trinity).

They are: Parā along with Her consort Bhairavasadbhava, Paraparã with Ratisekhara Bhairava & Apara with Navātmabhairava as revealed in the tradition's central text, the Malini-Vijayotarra Tantra.

In one sense, these three deities along with their consort are fierce, cremation ground energies visualized as being seated on lotus flowers & mounted atop a trident whose three prongs emerge from the sadãsiva's navel which in this visualization is synonymous with the brahmarandhra, the crown of the head.

While these three energies can be contacted and worshipped externally, as indeed they were by the early Käpâlikas of our tradition, in the later Kaula interpretation, they are seen as interior energies of Consciousness representing the Transcendent, the Transcendent-Immanent and the Immanent aspects of Reality.

In other words, these three are Goddesses as well as nodal points in the eternal triad of knower-knowing-known (which is itself a subtle re-interpretation of the classical Saiva triad of pati-pasa-pasu).

As such, rather than thinking of them as Goddesses, we can think of them as "stations" or "principles" and thereby plug in any deities we want like Ramakrishna-Sarada-Vivekananda or Sarasvati-Laxmi-Käli which is the the triad that appears in the sapta-sati of the Devi Mähâtmyam.
