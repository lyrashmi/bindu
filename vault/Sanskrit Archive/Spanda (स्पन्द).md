The pulse of parāvāc. Vibration caused by divine play ([[Līlā (लीला)]]), that results in emanation and represents the 5 great acts of Lord [[Śiva (शिव)]]. Spanda is energy, spanda is Śakti and comprises all of manifested reality. May also be referred to as the vibrationless vibration in its unmanifest aspect.

The pulsation of the absolute shiva, which is not a directional flowing, but rather a recognition of its simultaneous presence and transcendence.

Within spanda there is no temporal succession. There is just this intrinsic blossoming ([[Unmeṣa-Nimeṣa (उन्मेष-निमेष)|unmesha]]) of the potentialities of the absolute totality itself. Potency. It has simultaneously the potential to unfold into the manifold and still transcend its manifoldness and experience itself in this singular form. Shakti is the power which makes this possible ([[Vimarśa (विमर्श)]]). Shakti expresses itself in its [[Krama (क्रम)|five-fold structure]], there is no sequence within its expression either.

Within iccha there is one parā (the absolute), within jnana there is both action and volition (parāparā) merged, then we have kriya (the external dynamism, thrust, corporeality), there we have aparā. In other terminology we also have aghora, ghoraghoratara, ghora - etc. 

First the thrust of Vimarsha which is pure volition becomes jnana, which is a merge of both action and volition and that finally makes its way onto agency and finally becomes external action.

Within the expression/surge of the expression/manifestation (unmesha) of the colour blue there is this zeal towards blue and within that there is the dissolution (nimesha) of the colour yellow. This explains the surge of consciousness.

When there is no subjectivity and no foundation for objectivity, that doesn't means that the foundation is erased. It is spanda. The self is not the subject. It's a separate category. Recognise [[Bhūmi (भूमि)]]; and we can only do that if we recognise the rise of objectivity and subjectivity.

The paramārtha (ultimate nature) of Shiva is the way it manifests, the jagat is not distinct from Shiva. Even unmesha and nimesha have that nature.
