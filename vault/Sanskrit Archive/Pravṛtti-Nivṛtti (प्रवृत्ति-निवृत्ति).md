(withdrawal, cessation) // (worldy action, active participation) vs (withdrawal, detachment) // Chandika transcends them both == Padarthas:
Vidya (the wisdom/knowledge that reveals itself), Avidya (that imposes bondage on the Self), Paśu (the bound ones, individuated beings), Pāśa (bondage), Nivritti (the removal of Pāśa). These are six categories. Within this what is Pati? (from Pashupati): It is the one who grants bogha (enjoyment in outside world) and mukti/ laya(to dissolve within himself) (liberation). So pashupati is the Lord (the only one with agikara; authority) who bestows enjoyment and liberation to the pashus. That very self has the aspects of kriya and jnana, both of these fall under the domain of [[Vidyā (विद्या)]]. All the dharmas that are manifested in Pati are the dharmas called Shakti, all the Shaktis are intrinsic properties of Shiva, they are also considered vidya (the second category). Anything that falls under the domain of maya, starting from maya itself they are under the domain of avidya (ignorance), the third category. Our freedom is conditioned by our habit patterns, by mala (knowledge and karma (remember quote from tripura rahasya; "the biggest illusion is that knowledge isn't an illusion")), the one who is conditioned is called Pashu, who enjoys the world in the limited field (bhoga; based on dharma/adharma, as in virtue/vice) (this is the fifth state), and we aim for the liberation from Pasha. The sixth padartha (category) is the liberating factor, like mantra, mudra etc (Nivritti).
