The navadurgas (9 durgas) have different structures: 
3 kalpas (traditional method of performing durga puja over the span of 3 days, not to be confused with the billions year timespans): 1) Ugrachanda kalpa (more elaborate) 2) Bhadrakali kalpa (more mild) 3) Katyayani kalpa (mildest)
The 9th form of Navadurga is always visualised with 18 arms, the 8 petal goddesses around her are each visualised with 16 arms.
The upcoming 8 Chandis constitute the 8 petals, the 8 forms in which at the centre is Mahachandeshyogeshwari (or Ugrachanda herself), worshipped within Ugrachanda kalpa. Sometimes, in some more complex rituals while Ugrachanda is worshipped at the centre, Rudrachanda is worshipped at the periphery ([[Bhūpura (भूम्पुर)]]). 
In some rituals we invoke them in this order (within the 8 petals):
- Rudrachanda (16 arms, red-ish brown, east)
- Prachanda: Agnivarna; the goddess in the form of fire (16 arms, south-east)
- Chandroga (16 arms, dark/blackish hue, south)
- Chandonayika (16 arms, blue, south-west) 
- Chanda (16 arms, white, west)
- Chandavati (16 arms, smoky colour, north-west) 
- Chandarupa (16 arms, yellow, north)
- Atichanda (16 arms, white/yellow, north-east)
The difference between these goddesses is the change of the order of the weapons.
The [[Aśtamātṝkā (अष्टमातृका)]] according to the texts are subsumed into Chandi. Equally, in general it is assumed that since Ugrachanda sits at the middle and all other durgas are subsumed within her (i.e. navadurga simply being at the centre), that equally all the [[Mātṛkā (मातृका)|matrikas]] that are surrounding are subsumed to their corresponding durga. Which one however is currently unclear, as there is the need for more textual sources for confirming this.

Bhadrakali is the most gentle of the kalis, but she is still kali. She is known for destroying Raktabīja (the demon whose blood drops would create a whole new version of himself), by drinking the blood drops (she’s also known as Chamunda).
While Katyayani is the most benign form of the goddess, even more gentle than bhadrakali. In the uttaraamnaya (the northern transmission of Guhyakali) Katyayani is invoked as a most ferocious version, wearing human skulls and residing in the cremation ground, making the sound of Hoom. She comes when you call her, but then goes back to her own primordial slumber; she’s nidrā; she doesn’t cause the slumber, she is the slumber.
