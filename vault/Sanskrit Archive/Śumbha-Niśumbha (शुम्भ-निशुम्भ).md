Demons. Brilliance and non-brilliance.
See: [[Devīmahātmyam (देवीमहात्म्य)]]
When shumba says “it is not fair, I am just one and you are so many”, the goddess expresses the most pure form of aham (pūrna ahamta): I am the totality; “What is there other than myself?” she says to shumba - leading to the final realisation. 

The sense of I-ness and My-ness that are Shumbha (brilliance) and Nishumbha (non-brilliance), from the Devimahatmya (these qualities are the two sides of same coin and hence they are brothers). 

Nishumba was slain first. Ni-shumbha (ni as the negation, lit. ‘without brilliance’; doubt, or the ego’s inability to recognise ones own divinity and the truth of existence). Shumbha lastly (lit. ‘brilliance, to adorn’; superficial brilliance, symbolising external pride, arrogance, possessive tendencies [the spiritual ego?]).
